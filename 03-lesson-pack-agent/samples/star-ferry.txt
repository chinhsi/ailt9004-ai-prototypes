The Star Ferry

Every day, thousands of people cross Victoria Harbour on the Star Ferry. The green and white boats have carried passengers between Hong Kong Island and Kowloon since 1898. Back then, a ticket cost a few cents, and the ferry was the only way to cross the water. Today there are tunnels and trains, but many people still choose the ferry.

Why? First, it is cheap. A trip costs less than a cup of milk tea. Second, the view is famous. From the open deck you can see the tall buildings on both sides of the harbour, and at night the lights reflect on the water. Tourists take photos, while office workers read the news on their phones and pretend not to notice.

The crew wear blue sailor uniforms. When the ferry arrives, a sailor throws a thick rope to the pier and pulls the boat in. The gangway goes down with a loud bang, and everyone hurries off. The whole trip takes less than ten minutes.

Some people say the ferry is slow and old-fashioned. Others say that is exactly the point. In a city that never stops, a ferry ride is a short pause: a few minutes of wind, waves and open sky between two busy shores.
