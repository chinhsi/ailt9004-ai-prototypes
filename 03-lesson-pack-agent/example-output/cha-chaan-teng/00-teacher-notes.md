# Teacher Notes: Morning at the Cha Chaan Teng (茶餐廳的早晨)

## 1. Target Level and Rationale

- **Target Level:** Key Stage 3 (Secondary 1–3 / Grades 7–9 in Hong Kong).
- **Text Characteristics:** A descriptive narrative rich in sensory details (sight, smell, sound, taste), authentic Hong Kong cultural context, and nuanced reflections on urban life rhythms.
- **Pedagogical Rationale:** The text provides an accessible entry point to local Hong Kong literature and cultural themes. At the KS3 level, students develop skills in identifying sensory descriptions, inferring interpersonal dynamics (such as community rapport between customers and staff), and examining contrasting moods (the peak rush vs. subsequent tranquility).

---

## 2. Suggested 40-Minute Lesson Flow

| Time | Stage | Resource / Activity | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| **00–08 min** | **Pre-reading & Vocabulary Warm-up** | `01-vocabulary.md` | Elicit students' morning routines and visits to local diners. Introduce the 9 target vocabulary items (e.g., 出爐, 熟客, 照舊, 湧, 人潮). Students complete the 5 sentence-completion practice items in pairs. |
| **08–20 min** | **Guided Reading & Comprehension** | `02-comprehension.md` | Read the passage aloud as a class. Work through literal questions (Q1–4) quickly to verify factual understanding. Transition to group discussions for inferential questions (Q5–7), examining the implied bond between the waiter and Mr. Chan, and the lively imagery of Hong Kong morning rush. |
| **20–30 min** | **Consolidation Cloze Activity** | `03-cloze.md` | Have students independently complete the 10-gap cloze exercise without referring back to the original text initially. Review answers together to reinforce collocation, syntax, and vocabulary recall. |
| **30–38 min** | **Formative Assessment** | `04-quiz.html` | Students open the offline interactive quiz on tablets or classroom computers. The self-checking interface allows learners to immediately review explanations and assess their grasp of vocabulary and content. |
| **38–40 min** | **Plenary & Reflection** | Question 8 of `02-comprehension.md` | Conclude with an open sharing on the personal-response question: reflecting on the value of "ten minutes of peace after a storm of busyness" in school life. |

## 3. Differentiation Tips

- **For Weaker Students:**
  - **Vocabulary Scaffolding:** Provide visual aids or realia photos (e.g., freshly baked pineapple buns, cha chaan teng ticket slips) alongside `01-vocabulary.md`.
  - **Targeted Reading Clues:** In `02-comprehension.md`, direct students to the specific paragraph numbers corresponding to each literal question.
  - **Paired Cloze:** Allow students to complete `03-cloze.md` in mixed-ability pairs with the word bank highlighted.

- **For Stronger Students:**
  - **Creative & Analytical Writing Extension:** Ask students to expand on Ah Keung's perspective during the "ten minutes of quiet" (忙完之後那十分鐘的安靜) in a 100-character descriptive paragraph using at least three sensory details.
  - **Cultural Contrast Analysis:** Prompt students to compare the human warmth and tacit understanding ("照舊") of traditional cha chaan tengs with automated mobile-ordering or modern fast-food chains.

## 4. Check Before Use Checklist

Teachers should verify the following points prior to conducting the lesson:

1. **Cultural Context Familiarity:** Verify if all students (especially non-Chinese speaking or newly arrived students) are familiar with signature Hong Kong cha chaan teng dishes (菠蘿包, 絲襪奶茶, 沙嗲牛肉麵) and colloquially embedded service terms like "伙計" and "照舊".
2. **Device & Browser Compatibility:** Test opening `04-quiz.html` on the school's local devices (tablets, Chromebooks, or PC lab) to ensure local HTML file opening is permitted and rendering properly offline.
3. **Pacing Adjustment:** If your school runs 35-minute or 50-minute periods, adjust the cloze exercise (`03-cloze.md`) as homework or flipped pre-lesson preparation.
4. **Target Keystage Alignment:** Ensure the lexical depth matches your specific cohort's proficiency; for advanced Primary 6 or early Secondary 1, pre-teaching the character "湧" and idiom-like usages may be helpful.
